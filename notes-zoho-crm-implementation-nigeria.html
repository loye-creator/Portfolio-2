FIELD NOTE 04 / CRM IMPLEMENTATION

Zoho CRM implementation in Nigeria: cost, timeline and what actually goes wrong.

Most Nigerian businesses that buy a CRM are back on spreadsheets and WhatsApp within three months. The software is rarely the reason. This is how I scope a Zoho build so it is still in use a year later.

BY Okunola David Akinloye BASED Lagos and Abu Dhabi PUBLISHED 2 Nov 2026

The short answer. For most Nigerian SMEs, start on Zoho CRM Standard, budget three to six weeks for setup, and spend more of that time mapping how your team really sells than building. Connect WhatsApp early, keep required fields to a minimum, and name one person who owns the CRM after launch.

Why Zoho is the default choice for Nigerian SMEs

Three things make Zoho CRM the practical option for most small and mid-sized businesses here, ahead of HubSpot or Salesforce.

  • Naira billing. Zoho bills Nigerian customers in naira, which removes the domiciliary card problem and the monthly surprise when the exchange rate moves.
  • Price per seat. A five-person sales team on Standard costs a fraction of the equivalent HubSpot or Salesforce setup.
  • The rest of the suite. Zoho Mail, Campaigns, Books and Forms all connect without extra middleware, so a growing business can add pieces without rebuilding.

Microsoft Dynamics 365 is the stronger choice for larger organisations already living in Microsoft 365, and I implement both. For a team under fifty people, Zoho is usually the right call.

What Zoho CRM costs

There are two costs: the subscription, which you pay Zoho every month or year, and the implementation, which you pay once to get it set up properly.

The mistake I see most is buying Enterprise on day one "to be safe". A team that has never used a CRM needs the simplest version that matches how it sells. You can upgrade in an afternoon. You cannot get back three months of a team ignoring a system that was too complicated for them.

Data import is where timelines slip. Contacts scattered across phones, Excel files and WhatsApp exports need deduplicating and cleaning before they go in, or the CRM starts life full of rubbish and nobody trusts it.

The five reasons Nigerian teams stop using their CRM

1. The pipeline describes how you wish you sold

If the stages say "Qualified, Proposal, Negotiation" but deals here actually move through "DM, voice note, site visit, part payment", the team will not update it. Map stages from real deals, not from a template.

2. Too many required fields

Every required field is a reason to skip logging a lead. Start with name, phone, source and stage. Add fields only when someone can say what decision the data will change.

3. It is not connected to where leads arrive

Most enquiries in Nigeria arrive on WhatsApp and Instagram, not web forms. A CRM that needs a salesperson to copy a chat in by hand will be empty within weeks. Connecting WhatsApp, directly or through an automation layer like n8n, is not an extra. It is the build.

4. Nobody owns it after launch

A CRM drifts the moment the person who set it up leaves. Name one internal owner who checks the pipeline weekly and can make small changes without calling a consultant.

5. Training was one session

One demo on launch day does not change habits. Short follow-ups in the first month, run on the team's own live deals, do.

THE RULE

A CRM is adopted when updating it is easier than not updating it. Every setup decision should be tested against that sentence.

What this looked like on a real build

For TEPS, an events and experience company in Lagos, I implemented Zoho CRM Standard alongside their Meta campaigns for Wild West Throwdown 3.0, then documented the build and trained the team so it would survive without me. When TEPS brought me back in 2026, the next bottleneck was not the CRM itself but how leads reached it: every WhatsApp and Instagram enquiry went through one person. That became a separate automation build, which I wrote up in The one-person bottleneck.

For teams in the UAE

The same process applies to businesses in Abu Dhabi and Dubai, where Zoho is widely used by SMEs. The differences are mostly about language and channels: Arabic and English fields, more enquiries arriving by WhatsApp from international numbers, and VAT handling if you connect Zoho Books. I split my time between Lagos and Abu Dhabi and work with teams in both markets.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Zoho CRM cost in Nigeria?

There is a free plan for up to three users. Paid plans run from about $14 to $52 per user per month at US list prices, billed annually, and Zoho offers naira billing locally. Implementation is a separate, one-off cost.

How long does a Zoho CRM implementation take?

Three to six weeks for a small team with one sales process. Multiple pipelines, integrations or messy existing data make it longer.

Which plan should I start on?

Standard, for most SMEs. Move to Professional when you genuinely need inventory, approvals or heavier automation.

Can Zoho CRM connect to WhatsApp?

Yes, through Zoho's own integrations, marketplace extensions, or an automation layer such as n8n on the WhatsApp Business Platform.

Do I need a consultant, or can I set it up myself?

A small team with simple sales can set up Standard itself. A consultant earns their fee when there are several pipelines, existing data to clean, integrations to build, or a team that has already abandoned one CRM.
